# how to: export your ChatGPT data

To export your ChatGPT data, including chat history, you need to  **navigate to the Data Controls section within your ChatGPT settings and initiate the export process**. You'll receive an email with a link to download a ZIP file containing your data, including chat history in HTML format. [[1](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7260999-how-do-i-export-my-chatgpt-history-and-data),  [2](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8167885-how-to-export-your-data-from-the-chatgpt-android-app#:~:text=If%20you%20wish%20to%20export%20your%20chat,amount%20of%20data%20that%20is%20being%20requested.)]  
**Here's a step-by-step guide: [**[**1**](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7260999-how-do-i-export-my-chatgpt-history-and-data)**]**

1.  **Sign in to ChatGPT:**  Access your ChatGPT account through the OpenAI website.
2.  **Access Settings:**  Locate and click on your profile icon in the top right corner, then select "Settings".
3.  **Navigate to Data Controls:**  Within the settings, find and click on the "Data Controls" menu.
4.  **Initiate Export:**  Under "Export Data," click the "Export" button.
5.  **Confirm Export:**  In the confirmation screen, click "Confirm export".
6.  **Receive Email:**  You will receive an email with a link to download your data. Note that the link expires after 24 hours.
7.  **Download Data:**  Click on the "Download data export" link in the email to download a ZIP file containing your chat history and other data.

The downloaded ZIP file will include your chat history in HTML format (chat.html) and other data you've stored with OpenAI. [[1](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7260999-how-do-i-export-my-chatgpt-history-and-data)]

# how to: follow a wiki

To follow a wiki, you typically  **add specific pages or the entire wiki to your watchlist or subscribe to its feed**. This allows you to receive notifications when changes are made to the followed content. The exact method varies depending on the wiki platform, but common approaches include using "Watch" or "Follow" buttons on pages, subscribing to RSS or Atom feeds, or using a user's watchlist feature. [[1](https://help.hcl-software.com/connections/v55/user/wikis/t_wikis_track_changes.html),  [2](https://www.quora.com/Where-can-I-see-the-list-of-people-who-follow-a-Wikipedia-article),  [3](https://community.fandom.com/wiki/Help:Watchlist)]  
**Steps to follow a wiki (general):**

1.  **Find the wiki page or section you want to follow:**  Navigate to the specific page or section within the wiki that you want to track. [[1](https://help.hcl-software.com/connections/v55/user/wikis/t_wikis_track_changes.html),  [1](https://help.hcl-software.com/connections/v55/user/wikis/t_wikis_track_changes.html),  [2](https://www.quora.com/Where-can-I-see-the-list-of-people-who-follow-a-Wikipedia-article),  [2](https://www.quora.com/Where-can-I-see-the-list-of-people-who-follow-a-Wikipedia-article),  [4](https://www.scaler.com/topics/software-testing/wikis-and-watchlists-in-jira/#:~:text=Find%20the%20wiki%20page%20you%20want%20to,page%20title%20or%20the%20ellipsis%20menu%20%28&text=%29%20next%20to%20the%20page.)]
2.  **Locate the "Watch," "Follow," or similar feature:**  Many wikis provide a button or link, often found in the page header or toolbar, that allows you to add the page to your watchlist or follow it. [[1](https://help.hcl-software.com/connections/v55/user/wikis/t_wikis_track_changes.html),  [1](https://help.hcl-software.com/connections/v55/user/wikis/t_wikis_track_changes.html),  [3](https://community.fandom.com/wiki/Help:Watchlist),  [3](https://community.fandom.com/wiki/Help:Watchlist)]
3.  **(Optional) Subscribe to feeds:**  If the wiki offers RSS or Atom feeds, you can subscribe to them using your preferred feed reader. This will give you updates whenever the content is changed. [[1](https://help.hcl-software.com/connections/v55/user/wikis/t_wikis_track_changes.html),  [1](https://help.hcl-software.com/connections/v55/user/wikis/t_wikis_track_changes.html),  [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom_%28web_standard%29),  [6](https://www.stuff.co.nz/technology/digital-living/3061570/Guide-to-RSS-feeds#:~:text=You%20can%20then%20subscribe%20to%20receive%20the,will%20have%20different%20instructions%20for%20doing%20this.)]
4.  **Check your watchlist or notification settings:**  Depending on the wiki platform, you may need to configure your notification settings to receive updates about the content you are following. [[3](https://community.fandom.com/wiki/Help:Watchlist),  [3](https://community.fandom.com/wiki/Help:Watchlist)]

**Example: Following a page on a Fandom wiki:**

-   On a Fandom wiki, you can click the "Watch" button at the bottom of the page to add it to your watchlist.
-   You can then view your watchlist at Special:Watchlist to see a list of all the pages you are following.
-   You can also manage your watchlist and notification settings in your user preferences. [[3](https://community.fandom.com/wiki/Help:Watchlist)]

# how to: small web

The term "Small Web ecosystem" refers to a movement and approach to web development that is in contrast to the current "Big Web" dominated by large corporations  
It advocates for a web where individuals own and control their own online spaces, promoting decentralization, independence, and a focus on human-centric design.  
**Key Aspects of the Small Web Ecosystem:**

-   **Decentralization:**  Unlike the centralized "Big Web" where data and services are concentrated in the hands of large corporations, the Small Web favors a decentralized model with single-tenant servers. This means one server typically hosts one application serving one person.
-   **Individual Ownership and Control:**  A core principle is that individuals own and control their own websites and applications. This gives them greater control over their data and online experience, contrasting with the "Big Web" where users often rent space and forfeit control of their data.
-   **Human-Centered Design:**  The Small Web emphasizes creating websites and applications that are for people, not corporations or governments, and are built by individuals and small organizations. The aim is to make owning and managing one's online space easy and accessible without requiring technical expertise.
-   **Small Technology Principles:**  The Small Web applies principles of "Small Technology," which advocates for technologies that are sustainable, prioritize resource conservation, and are respectful of human dignity and autonomy.
-   **Focus on Relationships and Connection:**  The Small Web prioritizes genuine human connections and relationships over metrics like "engagement" and "virality" promoted by the "Big Web."
-   **Discovery and Serendipity:**  The Small Web encourages organic discovery through means like blogrolls and human-edited directories, in contrast to the algorithmically-driven recommendations of large platforms.

**Contrast with the "Big Web":**  
The "Big Web," as defined by Small Web advocates, is characterized by its centralized, corporate-owned infrastructure, reliance on surveillance capitalism, and a tendency to prioritize "engagement" metrics over genuine human connection.  
**The Goal:**  
The ultimate goal of the Small Web ecosystem is to create a digital public sphere made up of interconnected, individually-owned online spaces, offering an alternative to the dominant, centralized web. Organizations like the Small Technology Foundation are working to build the tools and infrastructure needed to make this vision a reality.
