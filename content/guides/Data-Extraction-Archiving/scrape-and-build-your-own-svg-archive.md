### TL;DR
*   **[The Metropolitan Museum of Art (The Met) Open Access](https://www.metmuseum.org/about-the-met/policies-and-documents/open-access)**: Best for high-res source imagery; you must convert these to SVG yourself.
*   **[Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Main_Page)**: Best for finding existing community-contributed SVG versions of classic motifs and iconography.
*   **[Vectorportal](https://www.vectorportal.com)**: A library of free-to-use vector files, often containing classic art motifs and historical patterns.
*   **[Inkscape](https://inkscape.org)**: The essential tool to convert your public domain high-res files into clean, scalable SVG paths.

---

### Sourcing & Creating Public Domain SVGs

*   **Why SVG is Rare for Fine Art**
    *   Classic paintings rely on color gradients, complex brushstrokes, and textural depth that are difficult to represent as "vector paths" without significant abstraction.
    *   Most "famous painting" SVGs you find will be **interpretations** (stylized or minimalist versions) rather than 1:1 reproductions.

*   **How to Build Your Own SVG Archive**
    1.  **Download High-Res Source**: Access [The Met Open Access](https://www.metmuseum.org/about-the-met/policies-and-documents/open-access) or [Art Institute of Chicago](https://www.artic.edu/open-access) to get public domain, ultra-high-resolution images.
    2.  **Vectorize**: Import these images into [Inkscape](https://inkscape.org) (free, open-source).
    3.  **Trace Bitmap**: Use the "Trace Bitmap" function to convert the raster painting into a set of vector paths.
    4.  **Simplify**: Manually clean up the resulting paths to keep your file sizes small and performant for your microsites.

*   **Recommended Repositories**
    *   **[Wikimedia Commons (SVG Category)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:SVG_paintings)**: Often hosts vector versions of specific symbolic art or simplified historical illustrations.
    *   **[Openclipart](https://openclipart.org)**: A community-driven archive where you can search for classic painting titles to see if a user has already created a vector tribute.

*   **Pro-Tip for Your Archive**
    *   If you are creating an "Old World" archive vibe, use **[Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/)** or **[Photopea](https://www.photopea.com)** to prepare your images (remove backgrounds/enhance contrast) before importing them into Inkscape for vectorization. This results in much cleaner, less "noisy" SVG code.
