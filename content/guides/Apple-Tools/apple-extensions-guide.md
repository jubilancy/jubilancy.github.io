TL;DR: Apple apps on macOS support various extensions that allow third-party software to integrate directly into the system or specific applications. While [Safari](https://support.apple.com/guide/safari/welcome/mac) is the most prominent user-facing example, many other system apps use extension frameworks to provide additional functionality.

- **[Finder](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/welcome/mac)**: Supports **Finder Sync** extensions, which allow apps (like Dropbox, Google Drive, or OneDrive) to display status badges on files and folders, and add custom commands to context menus (right-click).
    
- **[Photos](https://support.apple.com/guide/photos/welcome/mac)**: Uses **Photo Editing** extensions. These allow you to use professional image editing tools directly within the Photos app without having to export or import files, keeping your workflow inside the native library.
    
- **[Mail](https://support.apple.com/guide/mail/welcome/mac)**: Supports **Mail** extensions that allow apps to add features like email tracking, scheduling, custom templates, or security/encryption tools directly to the compose and inbox views.
    
- **Share Menu**: While not a single app, the **Share** extension is a system-wide framework. It appears in the "Share" button in almost every Apple app (Notes, Preview, Safari, etc.) and allows you to send content directly to other apps (like Reminders, Notes, or third-party social media tools).
    
- **Action Extensions**: These provide additional utility within apps by allowing you to process selected text, images, or files. You can typically find these in the "Share" or "Services" menu within most Mac applications.
    
- **Spotlight**: Supports extensions that allow third-party apps to return custom results when you search for items.
    
- **Audio Units**: Used by professional audio apps (like [GarageBand](https://www.apple.com/mac/garageband/) or [Logic Pro](https://www.apple.com/logic-pro/)) to add virtual instruments, effects, and sound processing tools.
    

### How to Manage Extensions

- **System Settings**: Most non-Safari extensions are managed via **System Settings > Extensions**. In this menu, you can toggle specific categories like **Finder**, **Share**, **Photos**, and **Actions**.
    
- **Application-Specific**: Some apps have their own internal settings for extensions (e.g., [Mail](https://support.apple.com/guide/mail/welcome/mac) has its own **Mail > Settings > Extensions** tab).
    
- **Discovery**: Unlike [Safari](https://support.apple.com/guide/safari/welcome/mac), there is no dedicated "Extension Store" for these other apps. They are usually installed as part of a full application; once you install the parent app (e.g., Dropbox for Finder sync, or Pixelmator for Photos editing), the extension becomes available in the corresponding system menu.

TL;DR: Apple Mail extensions are modular tools designed to enhance productivity and security within the [Mail](https://support.apple.com/guide/mail/welcome/mac) app. They are categorized by their specific functions:

- **Compose Extensions**: Assist in writing and sending emails. Examples include validating recipient addresses, adding custom headers, or providing access to email templates.
    
- **Action Extensions**: Help manage your inbox. They can perform tasks like automatically flagging messages or moving them to specific folders like Archive or Junk.
    
- **Content Blocker Extensions**: Enhance privacy by preventing the loading of remote content (such as tracking pixels/images) that could be used by senders to monitor your email activity.
    
- **Security Extensions**: Enable features like message encryption, decryption, and digital signing (often using technologies like PGP) to handle sensitive data securely.
    

### Managing Extensions

- **Installation**: Mail extensions are typically bundled with standalone apps found in the Mac App Store. They do not have a dedicated browsing section within the Mail app itself.
    
- **Activation**: After installing an app that contains an extension, you must enable it by going to **Mail > Settings > Extensions** and selecting the checkbox for the desired extension.
    
- **Usage**: Once enabled, extension icons may appear in the toolbar of your Mail viewer or Compose window. If an icon is not visible, you can add it by customizing the toolbar.
    
- **Popular Examples**: Apps like [Mailbutler](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mailbutler/id1313355438?mt=12) are well-known examples that utilize this framework to provide features like email tracking, scheduled sending, and collaboration tools.
