## Books like the Palliser novels

**Victorian political & parliamentary novels**

- [_Phineas Finn_ and the rest of the Palliser series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palliser_novels) aside, try Trollope's other work: [_The Way We Live Now_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Way_We_Live_Now) (Trollope's own savage satire on financial and social corruption)
- [_Coningsby_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coningsby) and [_Sybil_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sybil_\(novel\)) by [Benjamin Disraeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Disraeli) — a sitting PM writing political novels
- [_Middlemarch_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlemarch) by [George Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Eliot) — provincial political reform, ambition, and marriage as a study in character
- [_Felix Holt, the Radical_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Holt,_the_Radical) by George Eliot — election corruption and class politics

**Family saga / multi-generational society novels**

- [_The Forsyte Saga_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forsyte_Saga) by [John Galsworthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Galsworthy) — property, class, and family across generations, Edwardian follow-on to this tradition
- [_Vanity Fair_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_\(novel\)) by [William Makepeace Thackeray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Makepeace_Thackeray) — satire of social climbing, closest in tone to Trollope's picaresque and satiric edge
- [_Chronicles of Barsetshire_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicles_of_Barsetshire) — Trollope's own other series, ecclesiastical rather than parliamentary politics, same interconnected world

**Satire of institutions and corruptible power**

- [_Barchester Towers_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barchester_Towers) by Trollope — church politics as a mirror of parliamentary maneuvering
- [_Our Mutual Friend_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Mutual_Friend) by [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens) — money, status, and social hypocrisy
- [_Little Dorrit_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Dorrit) by Dickens — the Circumlocution Office is a direct satire of government incompetence and self-perpetuating bureaucracy

**Bildungsroman with a social/political dimension**

- [_Pendennis_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_History_of_Pendennis) by Thackeray — a young man's moral and social education
- [_Great Expectations_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Expectations) by Dickens — class ambition and disillusionment

**20th-century successors in the political-novel tradition**

- [_A Dance to the Music of Time_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Dance_to_the_Music_of_Time) by [Anthony Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Powell) — a 12-novel sequence tracking British society and power across decades
- [C.P. Snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._P._Snow)'s [_Strangers and Brothers_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangers_and_Brothers) sequence — institutional power, Whitehall and Westminster corridors
- [_The Politician_ / House of Cards trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Cards_\(novel\)) by [Michael Dobbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Dobbs,_Baron_Dobbs) — modern, cynical descendant of the parliamentary-intrigue genre

**Best sources for this kind of read-alike research**

- [Goodreads "Readers Also Enjoyed"](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/32261.Can_You_Forgive_Her_) — pull up any Palliser novel's page and scroll to that module
- [Goodreads Listopia](https://www.goodreads.com/list) — search lists like "Victorian Political Novels" or "Books like Trollope"
- [LibraryThing "Similar Books"](https://www.librarything.com/work/56935) — their algorithm is often better than Goodreads' for classic lit, and member reviews frequently make direct comparisons (you saw one in my search above, comparing Phineas Finn to Parnell-era politics)
- [NoveList Plus](https://www.ebscohost.com/novelist) (via a library login) — a readers'-advisory database built exactly for "if you liked X, try Y," with terms like "appeal factors" you can search on
- [Novelist's "read-alikes" via WhichBook](https://www.whichbook.net/) or [Literature Map](https://www.literature-map.com/) — type "Anthony Trollope" in and it plots visually similar authors by reader co-occurrence

**Search query patterns that actually work**

- `"readers also enjoyed" Trollope Palliser` (site-restrict to goodreads.com)
- `site:goodreads.com list Victorian political novels`
- `"if you liked Trollope"` or `"fans of Trollope"`
- `Trollope "read next" OR "readalikes"`
- `"parliamentary novel" Victorian fiction -Trollope` (to surface others in the same micro-genre)
- `"Barsetshire" OR "Palliser" comparable authors literary criticism`

**Academic/critical angle** — often the richest source for genuinely comparable books

- `"Trollope" "political novel" tradition scholarship`
- Search JSTOR or Google Scholar for `Trollope Palliser genre bildungsroman satire` — literary critics often place Trollope in a lineage (Thackeray → Trollope → Galsworthy → Powell) explicitly

## What the searches turned up

**Goodreads "Similar Authors" for Trollope directly recommends:**

- [_Barchester Towers_](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/374371) and [_The Warden_](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/20524) (Trollope's own Barsetshire series)
- [_The Way We Live Now_](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/20524) — repeatedly surfaced as his most acclaimed satire, 4.09 avg rating
- [_Doctor Thorne_](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/20524)

**A live Goodreads reader discussion** (in the [Victorians! group](https://www.goodreads.com/topic/show/217711-general-chit-chat-and-information-part-2)) put [_The Mill on the Floss_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mill_on_the_Floss) by George Eliot forward as a comparable pick — worth a look if you want the bildungsroman/family angle without the parliamentary politics.

**Scholarship confirms the critical lineage**: a [library catalog record](https://library.holycross.edu/Record/b1109907/Similar) shows Juliet McMaster's _Trollope's Palliser Novels: Theme and Pattern_ (1978) as the standard academic study, and — notably — [C.P. Snow wrote a full critical book titled simply _Trollope_](https://vufind.lboro.ac.uk/Record/64050/Similar), which lines up with my earlier suggestion that his own _Strangers and Brothers_ sequence descends from this tradition.

The Goodreads book pages themselves (like Can You Forgive Her?'s "Readers Also Enjoyed" carousel) are JavaScript-rendered, so I can't fetch that module directly — you'd get more there by opening [the book page](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/374371) yourself and scrolling to it.
