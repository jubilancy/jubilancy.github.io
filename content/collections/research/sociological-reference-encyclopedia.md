## I. Macro-Level (Structural) Theories
Focus on large-scale social structures, institutions, and systems.

### 1. Structural Functionalism
- **Key figures:** Émile Durkheim, Talcott Parsons, Robert Merton
- **Core idea:** Society is a system of interconnected parts (institutions) that work together to maintain stability and equilibrium, like organs in a body.
- **Key concepts:** Manifest/latent functions, dysfunction, social solidarity (mechanical vs. organic), anomie
- **Critique:** Overly static, ignores conflict and change, conservative bias

### 2. Conflict Theory
- **Key figures:** Karl Marx, Ralf Dahrendorf, C. Wright Mills
- **Core idea:** Society is characterized by inequality and conflict between groups competing for scarce resources (wealth, power, status)
- **Key concepts:** Class struggle, bourgeoisie/proletariat, false consciousness, power elite
- **Variants:**
  - Marxist/Class Conflict Theory
  - Weberian Conflict Theory (multiple bases of power: class, status, party)
  - Critical Race Theory (race as a central axis of conflict)

### 3. Feminist Theory
- **Key figures:** Dorothy Smith, Patricia Hill Collins, bell hooks
- **Core idea:** Gender is a central organizing principle of social life and a source of inequality
- **Key concepts:** Patriarchy, intersectionality, standpoint theory, gender socialization

### 4. World Systems Theory
- **Key figures:** Immanuel Wallerstein
- **Core idea:** Global economy divided into core, semi-periphery, and periphery nations with unequal exchange
- **Key concepts:** Core-periphery relations, global capitalism, dependency

### 5. Dependency Theory
- **Key figures:** Andre Gunder Frank, Fernando Henrique Cardoso
- **Core idea:** Underdevelopment in poorer nations results from their exploitative position in the global economic system

### 6. Postmodern/Poststructural Theory
- **Key figures:** Michel Foucault, Jean Baudrillard, Jean-François Lyotard
- **Core idea:** Rejects grand narratives; focuses on power, discourse, and the fragmented, constructed nature of social reality
- **Key concepts:** Discourse, power/knowledge, simulacra, deconstruction

## II. Micro-Level (Interactionist) Theories
Focus on individual and small-group interactions, meaning-making.

### 7. Symbolic Interactionism
- **Key figures:** George Herbert Mead, Herbert Blumer, Charles Horton Cooley
- **Core idea:** Society is created through everyday interactions and the shared meanings people attach to symbols (language, gestures, objects)
- **Key concepts:** The looking-glass self, the "I" and "me," definition of the situation

### 8. Dramaturgical Theory
- **Key figures:** Erving Goffman
- **Core idea:** Social life is like a theatrical performance; people manage impressions through "front stage" and "back stage" behavior
- **Key concepts:** Impression management, front/back stage, face-work, stigma

### 9. Ethnomethodology
- **Key figures:** Harold Garfinkel
- **Core idea:** Studies how people make sense of and produce the taken-for-granted order of everyday life
- **Key concepts:** Indexicality, reflexivity, breaching experiments

### 10. Social Exchange Theory
- **Key figures:** George Homans, Peter Blau, Richard Emerson
- **Core idea:** Human relationships are formed through cost-benefit analysis and the exchange of rewards/resources
- **Key concepts:** Rewards, costs, reciprocity, comparison level

### 11. Rational Choice Theory
- **Key figures:** James Coleman, Gary Becker
- **Core idea:** Individuals act to maximize personal benefit based on rational calculation of costs and benefits
- **Key concepts:** Utility maximization, self-interest, methodological individualism

### 12. Phenomenology
- **Key figures:** Edmund Husserl (philosophy), Alfred Schutz (sociology)
- **Core idea:** Focuses on subjective experience and how individuals construct meaning from lived experience
- **Key concepts:** Lifeworld, intersubjectivity, bracketing

## III. Meso-Level & Integrative Theories
Bridge structure and agency, or focus on mid-range social phenomena.

### 13. Structuration Theory
- **Key figures:** Anthony Giddens
- **Core idea:** Social structures both shape and are shaped by human agency (the "duality of structure")
- **Key concepts:** Agency, structure, recursiveness, practical consciousness

### 14. Field Theory / Theory of Practice
- **Key figures:** Pierre Bourdieu
- **Core idea:** Behavior is shaped by the interplay of habitus (internalized dispositions), capital (economic, social, cultural, symbolic), and field (social arena)
- **Key concepts:** Habitus, capital, field, distinction

### 15. Network Theory
- **Key figures:** Mark Granovetter, Barry Wellman
- **Core idea:** Social behavior is shaped by the structure of relationships (networks) individuals are embedded in
- **Key concepts:** Strength of weak ties, social capital, embeddedness

### 16. Institutional Theory
- **Key figures:** John Meyer, Paul DiMaggio, Walter Powell
- **Core idea:** Organizations and behaviors conform to institutionalized norms and rules to gain legitimacy
- **Key concepts:** Isomorphism (coercive, mimetic, normative), legitimacy

### 17. Rational Choice Institutionalism / New Institutionalism
- Combines rational choice with structural constraints of institutions

## IV. Critical & Culture-Focused Theories

### 18. Critical Theory (Frankfurt School)
- **Key figures:** Theodor Adorno, Max Horkheimer, Herbert Marcuse, Jürgen Habermas
- **Core idea:** Society shaped by capitalism and culture industries that manufacture consent and limit critical consciousness
- **Key concepts:** Culture industry, communicative action, false needs

### 19. Cultural Theory / Cultural Studies
- **Key figures:** Stuart Hall, Raymond Williams
- **Core idea:** Culture is a site of struggle over meaning, tied to power and ideology
- **Key concepts:** Hegemony, encoding/decoding, ideology

### 20. Queer Theory
- **Key figures:** Judith Butler, Eve Sedgwick
- **Core idea:** Challenges fixed categories of gender/sexuality as socially constructed and performative
- **Key concepts:** Performativity, heteronormativity, social construction of identity

### 21. Postcolonial Theory
- **Key figures:** Edward Said, Gayatri Spivak, Homi Bhabha
- **Core idea:** Examines the lasting cultural, political, and economic impacts of colonialism
- **Key concepts:** Orientalism, subaltern, hybridity

---

## V. Evolutionary & Biosocial Theories

### 22. Sociobiology / Evolutionary Sociology
- **Key figures:** E.O. Wilson
- **Core idea:** Social behavior has evolutionary/biological roots shaped by natural selection
- **Key concepts:** Kin selection, reciprocal altruism, adaptive behavior

### 23. Social Darwinism (historical)
- **Key figures:** Herbert Spencer
- **Core idea:** Applies "survival of the fittest" to social competition and stratification
- **Note:** Largely discredited/controversial, historically significant

---

## VI. Social Psychology-Adjacent Sociological Theories

### 24. Labeling Theory
- **Key figures:** Howard Becker, Edwin Lemert
- **Core idea:** Deviance is created by social reaction/labeling rather than being inherent to an act
- **Key concepts:** Primary/secondary deviance, master status, self-fulfilling prophecy

### 25. Social Learning Theory (Sociological application)
- **Key figures:** Albert Bandura (psych origin), Ronald Akers (sociological application)
- **Core idea:** Behavior is learned through observation, imitation, and reinforcement within social contexts
- **Key concepts:** Differential association, modeling, reinforcement

### 26. Differential Association Theory
- **Key figures:** Edwin Sutherland
- **Core idea:** Criminal/deviant behavior is learned through interaction with others who hold favorable definitions of that behavior

### 27. Social Control Theory
- **Key figures:** Travis Hirschi
- **Core idea:** People conform due to strong bonds to society (attachment, commitment, involvement, belief); weak bonds allow deviance

### 28. Strain Theory
- **Key figures:** Robert Merton (building on Durkheim's anomie)
- **Core idea:** Deviance results from a gap between culturally valued goals and the legitimate means to achieve them
- **Key concepts:** Conformity, innovation, ritualism, retreatism, rebellion

### 29. Differential Opportunity Theory
- **Key figures:** Richard Cloward, Lloyd Ohlin
- **Core idea:** Access to illegitimate means (not just legitimate ones) also varies, shaping deviant subcultures

## VII. Contemporary & Emerging Theories

### 30. Actor-Network Theory (ANT)
- **Key figures:** Bruno Latour, Michel Callon
- **Core idea:** Social phenomena result from networks involving both human and non-human actors (objects, technology)

### 31. Risk Society Theory
- **Key figures:** Ulrich Beck
- **Core idea:** Modern society is organized around managing risks produced by industrialization and modernization

### 32. Liquid Modernity
- **Key figures:** Zygmunt Bauman
- **Core idea:** Contemporary social life is characterized by constant change, instability, and fluid identities/relationships

### 33. Communicative Action Theory
- **Key figures:** Jürgen Habermas
- **Core idea:** Society is shaped by rational communication aimed at mutual understanding, contrasted with instrumental/strategic action

### 34. Intersectionality Theory
- **Key figures:** Kimberlé Crenshaw
- **Core idea:** Multiple social identities (race, gender, class, etc.) intersect to create unique, compounded experiences of oppression/privilege

### 35. Social Constructionism
- **Key figures:** Peter Berger, Thomas Luckmann
- **Core idea:** Reality and knowledge are actively constructed through social processes and shared understanding
- **Key concepts:** Externalization, objectivation, internalization
