## 1. Lexicography & Obscure Terms

*From Image 1: "Obscure Terms" OCR.*

- **lotus-eater** (noun) — A word with Greek mythology origins; a lotus-eater is characterized as one who lives their life in a constant state of dream and fantasy, completely indifferent to the busy world and its problems.
- **Jester's privilege** — The ability and right of a jester to talk and mock freely without being punished; for nothing he says seems to matter.
- **Belladonna of Sadness** — A graphic depiction of one woman's descent into hallucinatory madness as she is plagued by visions of evil spirits that possess her body and mind.
- **paracosm** (noun, Psychology) — [Wikipedia: Paracosm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracosm). An extremely rare word defining the imaginary world constructed in one's mind, specifically by children. It is an infinite fantasy — anything can exist, from animals to aliens and entities foreign to outsiders.
- **Cain complex** (n.) — A complex characterized by rivalry, competition, and extreme envy or jealousy of a brother, leading to hatred.
- **Got the morbs** (1880) — A Victorian era slang term/euphemism describing a person afflicted with temporary melancholy or sadness.
- **Shakespeare Insult Kit** — To create a Shakespearean insult, combine one word from each of three columns, prefaced with "Thou" (word lists were present in the original image but not transcribed in the OCR text).
- **hedgehog's dilemma** (also "porcupine dilemma") — A metaphor about the challenges of human intimacy. Describes a situation in which a group of hedgehogs seek to move close to one another to share heat during cold weather. They must remain apart, however, as they cannot avoid hurting one another with their sharp spines.

## 4. Mesoamerican Featherwork (Historical Source Material)

*From Image 3. Description of [Mesoamerican Featherwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Featherwork) and (some) pieces made by Aztec/Nahua, Purépecha, etc. artists for the Spanish.*

**Source quote — Bartolomé de las Casas, *Apologetic History*, tome I, book III, chapter LXII, 1536:**

> "What...appears to surpass all...ingenuity, and which... will strike...the world as novel...[and] should be... admired and prized, is the...art... those Mexican people know how to work... to such perfection, using... feathers and their... colours to the same excellence..."
>
> "These feathers might be green, orange, ruby... purple,... yellow, blue... and every other colour...undyed... all natural... and for that reason all species of bird were held in great value..."
>
> "...if depicting a living thing [or natural object] it would appear to come alive [and appear natural]...they showed such great subtlety of genius and... wondrous skills..."
>
> "...Before we Christians arrived... they were already working...this skill...[making] marvellous... art...after [our] arrival... they saw our images and altarpieces... [and] our things and... [demonstrated] the sharpness of their understanding..."

**Captioned pieces:**
- Saint John the Evangelist, 17th century — Daniel Liebsohn Collection
- Saint Peter, 17th century — Museo de Historia Mexicana
- Life of Saint John the Baptist OR Christ the Good Shepherd, Juan Baptista Cuiris(?), 16th century — Musée du Quai Branly
- Featherwork Image of Christ, Juan Baptista Cuiris, 16th century — Kunsthistorisches Museum
- Saint Michael Slaying the Devil, 16th century — Museum of Fine Arts Boston
