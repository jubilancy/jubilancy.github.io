# psych

## identitarian victimhood culture:

- [Victimhood as Status: Evidence from Politics and Religion](https://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/pspp0000400)  
  Explores how adopting a victimhood identity can enhance status and moral authority.

- [The Rise of Victimhood Culture](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0003122414552284)  
  Foundational paper by Campbell and Manning introducing the concept of "victimhood culture" as a moral framework.

- [Identitarianism and the Construction of Victimhood in the Far-Right](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/08969205231156994)  
  Analyzes how far-right identitarian movements frame themselves as victims to legitimize exclusionary politics.

- [The Therapeutic Ethos and the Emergence of Victimhood Culture](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1468-4446.12019)  
  Considers how therapeutic discourse contributes to the valorization of victim identity in Western societies.

- [The Cult of the Victim](https://www.city-journal.org/html/cult-victim-12234.html)  
  A polemical yet influential essay discussing how victimhood is weaponized in contemporary cultural politics.

- [The Rise of Identity Politics: An Empirical Study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/rise-of-identity-politics-an-empirical-study-of-cultural-framing/20AFC9B03C3B9BE57053501F44C9160F)  
  Charts the increasing salience of identity-based grievances in political life.

- [Postcolonial Victimhood: National Identity and the Politics of Remembrance](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv1q6dqhp)  
  Examines how victim narratives are mobilized within identity politics and postcolonial nation-building.

- [How Identity Politics Became Victim Politics](https://americanaffairsjournal.org/2019/08/how-identity-politics-became-victim-politics/)  
  Traces the shift from civil rights to grievance-based politics in American liberalism.

- [The Psychology of Victimhood](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02903/full)  
  A review of how perceived victim status influences interpersonal and intergroup behavior.

- [Moral Cultures: Honor, Dignity, and Victimhood](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281433242_Microaggression_and_Moral_Cultures)  
  Elaborates on the distinctions between honor, dignity, and victimhood cultures, emphasizing their societal conditions.

## female vs. male expressions of toxicity; examining gendered behavior patterns, relational aggression, and sociocultural dynamics:

- [Gender Differences in Aggression](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/1467-8721.01232)  
  Reviews literature on how men and women differ in expressions of aggression—men more physical, women more relational.

- [Relational Aggression in Girls](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1086/428417)  
  Focuses on indirect, social, and reputational forms of aggression common in female peer groups.

- [Toxic Femininity: The Dark Side of Women’s Behavior](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357421226_Toxic_Femininity_The_Dark_Side_of_Women's_Behavior)  
  Examines manipulative, controlling, or emotionally exploitative behavior within gendered frameworks.

- [Toxic Masculinity: A Structural Understanding](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1097184X20943254)  
  Analyzes toxic masculinity as a systemic phenomenon, focusing on power, dominance, and emotional repression.

- [The Covert Aggression of Women](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-human-equation/202111/the-covert-aggression-women)  
  A popular-psychology breakdown of how toxic behavior among women is often socially disguised.

- [Mean Girls and Social Hierarchies](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/development-and-psychopathology/article/abs/female-social-aggression-and-popularity/E1C621DC69B63A93E21E3BAEF9E6B062)  
  Investigates popularity and aggressive social strategies among adolescent girls.

- [Toxic Behavior in Online Gaming: Gender Differences](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/15213269.2019.1650139)  
  Studies toxic behavior by gender in digital social environments, with men exhibiting more overt harassment and women more passive-aggressive tactics.

- [The Gendered Nature of Emotional Manipulation](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0959353510384151)  
  Explores how emotional manipulation is differentially gendered and perceived in intimate relationships.

- [Toxic Femininity on TikTok: Soft Violence and the Aestheticization of Harm](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/20563051231170571)  
  Discusses social media’s role in veiling toxic behavior under femininity and empowerment.

- [Hegemonic Femininity and the Myth of “Nice Girls”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0959353516678014)  
  Challenges perceptions of women’s toxicity as rare or innocuous, tying it to dominant feminine ideals.

# philosophy games

**Video games:**  Game Title (2023 cost, minimum playtime before discussion). _Prices are pre-sale prices. I recommend purchasing the games during Steam summer and winter sales._  
[Among Us](https://store.steampowered.com/app/945360/Among_Us/)  ($5, 30 mins) This is a social deduction game where players are given roles of either crewmate or imposter. The imposters' objective is to kill all the crewmates, and the crewmates' objective is to find and vote out the imposter. The game involves a lot of critical thinking and group deliberation. It can be used to help students see biases, heuristics, and reasoning patterns in the wild.  [I use this game as the basis of my midterm in my critical thinking course.](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Z9FsjgNXOkCLjnNnxr_Uwg8O6fskhk8S/view)  [Critical Thinking, Logic]  
[Before Your Eyes](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1082430/Before_Your_Eyes/)  ($10, 1 hour) This game presents players with short scenes that change every time a player blinks (this game uses the player's webcam for eye tracking). It uses this mechanic to explore the passage of time and the meaning of life. [Phenomenology of time, meaning of life, narrative views of self]  
[Doors, Inc.](https://www.doors.gua-le-ni.com/)  (Free, 30 mins) A point-and-click browser game about representation of doors. It focuses on fictional representations of doors in video games. This makes it great for an aesthetics class, if you are discussing fiction and representation. However, it could also be great to use in an intro to metaphysics, mind, or epistemology class. The art is great and the writing is fun. The game contains further readings in aesthetics that could be paired with the game. [Aesthetics, Metaphysics, Mind, Epistemology, Representation and Reality] Here is Nele Van de Mosselaer, a philosopher and one of the game's designers, on discussing the idea for the game: [https://junkyardofthemind.com/blog/2024/9/6/doors-an-experiment-in-playable-philosophy](https://junkyardofthemind.com/blog/2024/9/6/doors-an-experiment-in-playable-philosophy)  
[Everything](https://store.steampowered.com/app/582270/Everything/)  ($14, 30 mins) -The game has you embody different creatures and form groups to explore ideas about the interconnectivity of nature, the value and meaning of life, and society. The game is inspired by Alan Watts and includes short recordings of his talks. [Meaning of life, Eastern philosophy]  
[Getting Over it](https://store.steampowered.com/app/240720/Getting_Over_It_with_Bennett_Foddy/)  ($8, 30 mins) - The game has players attempt to climb up a mountain using only a hammer to pull and push themselves up. Any mistake leads the player right back at the beginning. Great for teaching existentialism and the myth of Sisyphus. Additionally, there are youtube videos of people completing this game in only a few minutes. This could be an interesting game to discuss skill and know-how [Existentialism, Meaninglessness of life, know-how]  
[Minecraft](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us)  ($30, depends on) MInecraft is a sandbox survival game where many players can make bases and interact in an expansive world. The sandbox nature of the game allows you to create many kinds of simulations. [An excellent example is Rebeccah Leiby's 10-day summer course on Hobbes' political philosophy](https://academics.hamilton.edu/philosophysummerschool/hcspip-2023/the-state-of-nature-ultimate-survival-mode).  
[Only Up](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2381590/Only_Up/)  ($8, 30 mins) This game has players navigate a difficult obstacle course that leads them up into the sky. When a player falls, they fall back to the beginning. The game starts in the favelas and represents this climb upwards as movement up the social ladder. [Social mobility]  
[Papers Please](https://store.steampowered.com/app/239030/Papers_Please/)  ($10, 30 mins) This game has players occupy the role of a border agent in an authoritarian society. Players are presented with many difficult moral decisions, such as whether to let in people with fake passports who are immigrating for medical care, and whether to support a resistance movement against the government. [Political philosophy, responsibility]  
[Peacemaker](http://peacemakergame.com/)  (Free, ~45 minutes): I use this game to teach jus ad bellum. The game has students play as either the leader of Israel or the leader of the Palestinian Authority. The goal of the game is to earn enough good will from both Israelis and Palestianians to create a peaceful two state solution. To do this, players must take actions and Israelis, Palestinians, and other international groups and states will react. I have students play on the hardest difficulty to help them appreciate the difficulty of managing the political situation while evaluating their decisions from a moral perspective. [Ethics of war]  
[Silicon Dreams](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1272070/Silicon_Dreams____cyberpunk_interrogation/)  ($15, 30 mins, in class option) This game has you play as an android whose job is to interrogate other androids and determine whether they ought to be decommissioned. This requires evaluating their emotional responses to questions you ask to determine whether they are operating normally. This game could be played in front of a class, with students voting on which result they think ought to be selected. This game covers similar themes as the movie "Blade Runner," such as personhood and rights of human-like AI. However, it also reflects on the normativity of role-based obligations, similar to "Papers Please." [Philosophy of AI, Philosophy of Mind, Normativity]  
[Stanley Parable](https://store.steampowered.com/app/221910/The_Stanley_Parable/)  ($15, 30 mins) A very funny game about an employee trying to escape from his office while the narrator imposes narratives on your actions and exercises control over what you can do. The game does a great job representing the feeling of being locked in to a certain life path despite being "free" by using video game logic and a funny voiceover control a player's choices, despite giving them freedom of movement. The game involves "starting over" to try different paths (this is included in the 30 minutes), so make sure students don't stop after their first attempt. [Free will, Existentialism (Sartre on freedom)]  
[Talos Principle](https://store.steampowered.com/app/257510/The_Talos_Principle/)  ($30, ~1 hour): This game has you play as a robot solving puzzles designed by a deity who seems to rule the strange universe you find yourself in.The prize for solving the puzzles are shapes, which can be used to unlock further puzzles and climb a tower. The deity says that these shapes are inherently valuable and orders you not to climb the tower. The game could be used to discuss the Euthyphro Problem with around 1 hour of playtime (students should aim to at least ride the first elevator up to ground level). The game also explores personhood, transhumanism, philosophy of religion, and the philosophy of technology, but reaching these parts of the game would require significantly more playtime and there is no guarantee that all students will see the same content. [Metaethics, Euthyphro problem, philosophy of religion, personhood, philosophy of technology]  
[The Moral Machine](https://www.moralmachine.net/)  (Free, 5 minutes): This is a project to gather data on people's judgments about ethical choices that self-driving cars might need to make in emergency situations. I use this game early in the semester to get them thinking about morally relevant differences. [Normative ethics, applied ethics]  
[The Swapper](https://store.steampowered.com/app/231160/The_Swapper/)  ($15, ?) This game has players creating clones of themselves to solve puzzles. The game can be used as a thought experiment to explore topics about survival and the metaphysics of personal identity. Similar to the movie "The Prestige." [Personal identity]  
[This War of Mine](https://store.steampowered.com/app/282070/This_War_of_Mine/)  ($20, ?) This game has you play as a group of civilians surviving in a city at the front lines of a war. The game explores the ethics and reality of warfare. [Ethics in wartime]  
[The Witness](https://store.steampowered.com/app/210970/The_Witness/)  ($40, 1-2 hours) This game has players solving maze-like puzzles. Solutions to these puzzles quickly require a lot of creativity. This game could be used to explore intuition and insight in an epistemology class. [intuition, insight]  
[Universal Paperclips](https://www.decisionproblem.com/paperclips/)  (Free, 1 hour) This game has you creating and selling paperclips, gradually automating the process. The game is inspired by the "paperclip maximizer" AI doomsday scenario. [Philosophy of AI]
