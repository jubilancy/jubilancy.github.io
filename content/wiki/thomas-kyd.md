**Thomas Kyd** (baptised 6 November 1558; buried 15 August 1594) was an English [playwright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playwright "Playwright"), the author of _[The Spanish Tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spanish_Tragedy "The Spanish Tragedy")_, and one of the most important figures in the development of [Elizabethan drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabethan_theatre "Elizabethan theatre").

Although well known in his own time, Kyd fell into obscurity until 1773 when [Thomas Hawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hawkins_(literary_editor) "Thomas Hawkins (literary editor)"), an early editor of _The Spanish Tragedy_, discovered that [Thomas Heywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Heywood "Thomas Heywood"), in his _Apologie for Actors_ (1612), attributed the play to Kyd. A hundred years later, scholars in Germany and England began to shed light on his life and work, including the controversial finding that he may have been the author of a _Hamlet_ play pre-dating Shakespeare's, which is now known as the _[Ur-Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ur-Hamlet "Ur-Hamlet")_.

## Early life

Thomas Kyd was the son of Francis and Anna Kyd. There are no records of the day he was born, but he was baptised in the church of [St Mary Woolnoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary_Woolnoth "St Mary Woolnoth") in the Ward of Langborn, Lombard Street, London on 6 November 1558. The baptismal register at St Mary Woolnoth carries this entry: "Thomas, son of Francis Kydd, Citizen and Writer of the Courte Letter of London". Francis Kyd was a [scrivener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrivener "Scrivener") and in 1580 was warden of the [Scriveners' Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scriveners'_Company "Scriveners' Company").

In October 1565 the young Kyd was enrolled in the newly founded [Merchant Taylors' School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_Taylors'_School,_Northwood "Merchant Taylors' School, Northwood"), whose headmaster was [Richard Mulcaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Mulcaster "Richard Mulcaster"). Fellow students included [Edmund Spenser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Spenser "Edmund Spenser") and [Thomas Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Lodge "Thomas Lodge"). Here, Kyd received a well-rounded education, with the curriculum including Italian, Latin, Greek, music, drama, physical education, and "good manners". There is no evidence that Kyd went on to university. He may have followed in his father's professional footsteps because there are two letters written by him where his handwriting style is similar to that of a scrivener.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-:1-1)

## Career

[![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Kyd-SpanishTragedie-title.JPG/500px-Kyd-SpanishTragedie-title.JPG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Kyd-SpanishTragedie-title.JPG) Title page of Kyd's _[The Spanish Tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spanish_Tragedy "The Spanish Tragedy"),_ with a woodcut showing (left) the hung body of Horatio discovered by (centre) [Hieronymo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieronymo "Hieronymo"); and [Bel-imperia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel-imperia "Bel-imperia") being taken from the scene by a [blackface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackface "Blackface") Lorenzo (right).

Evidence suggests that in the 1580s Kyd became an important playwright, but little is known about his activity. [Francis Meres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Meres "Francis Meres") placed him among "our best for tragedy" and Heywood elsewhere called him "Famous Kyd". [Ben Jonson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Jonson "Ben Jonson") mentions him in the same breath as [Christopher Marlowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Marlowe "Christopher Marlowe") (with whom, in London, Kyd at one time shared a room) and [John Lyly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lyly "John Lyly") in the [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare "Shakespeare") First Folio.

_[The Spanish Tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spanish_Tragedy "The Spanish Tragedy")_ was probably written in the mid to late 1580s, with its first recorded performance on 23 February 1592 by [Lord Strange's Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Strange's_Men "Lord Strange's Men").[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-:0-2) The earliest surviving edition was printed in 1592, the full title being _The Spanish Tragedie, Containing the lamentable end of Don Horatio, and Bel-imperia: with the pittifull death of olde Hieronimo_. However, the play was usually known simply as "Hieronimo" after the [protagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protagonist "Protagonist"). It was arguably the most popular play of the "Age of Shakespeare" and set new standards in effective plot construction and character development. There were "twenty-nine performances between 1592 and 1597" and "eleven editions between 1592 and 1633", which the historian J. R. Mulryne states is "a tally unequaled by any of the plays of Shakespeare".[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-:1-1) In 1602 a version of the play with "additions" was published. [Philip Henslowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Henslowe "Philip Henslowe")'s diary records payment to Ben Jonson for additions that year, but it is disputed whether the published additions reflect Jonson's work or if they were actually composed for a 1597 revival of _The Spanish Tragedy_ also mentioned by Henslowe.

Other works by Kyd are his translations of [Torquato Tasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torquato_Tasso "Torquato Tasso")'s _Padre di Famiglia_, published as _The Householder's Philosophy_ (1588), and of [Robert Garnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Garnier "Robert Garnier")'s _[Cornélie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn%C3%A9lie_(play)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Cornélie (play)")_ (1594), along with the play _[Soliman and Perseda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soliman_and_Perseda?action=edit&redlink=1 "Soliman and Perseda")_. Plays disputedly attributed, in whole or in part, to Kyd include _[King Leir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Leir "King Leir")_, _[Fair Em](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Em "Fair Em")_, _[Arden of Faversham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arden_of_Faversham "Arden of Faversham")_ and parts of _[1 Henry VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VI,_Part_1 "Henry VI, Part 1")_ and _[Edward III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_III_(play) "Edward III (play)")_.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-3) A play related to _The Spanish Tragedy_ called _The First Part of Hieronimo_ (surviving in a quarto of 1605) may be a [bad quarto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_quarto "Bad quarto") or [memorial reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_reconstruction "Memorial reconstruction") of a play by Kyd, or it may be an inferior writer's burlesque of _The Spanish Tragedy_ inspired by that play's popularity.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-4) Kyd is supposed by some to have been the author of a _Hamlet_, the precursor of the Shakespearean play (see: _[Ur-Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ur-Hamlet "Ur-Hamlet")_).

The success of Kyd's plays extended to Europe. Versions of _The Spanish Tragedy_ were popular in Germany and the Netherlands for generations. The influence of these plays on European drama was largely the reason for the interest in Kyd among German scholars in the nineteenth century.

## Later life

From 1587 to 1593 Kyd was in the service of an unidentified noble, since, after his imprisonment in 1593 (see below), he wrote of having lost "the favours of my Lord, whom I haue servd almost theis vi yeres nowe". Proposed nobles include the [Earl of Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Radclyffe,_4th_Earl_of_Sussex "Henry Radclyffe, 4th Earl of Sussex"),[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-5) the [Earl of Pembroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Herbert,_2nd_Earl_of_Pembroke "Henry Herbert, 2nd Earl of Pembroke"),[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-6) [Lord Strange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinando_Stanley,_5th_Earl_of_Derby "Ferdinando Stanley, 5th Earl of Derby").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-7) and [Edward De Vere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_de_Vere,_17th_Earl_of_Oxford "Edward de Vere, 17th Earl of Oxford"), 17th Earl of Oxford. He may have worked as a secretary, if he did not also write plays. Around 1591 [Christopher Marlowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Marlowe "Christopher Marlowe") also joined this patron's service, and for a while Marlowe and Kyd shared lodgings, and perhaps even ideas.

On 11 May 1593 the [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom "Privy Council of the United Kingdom") ordered the arrest of the authors of "divers lewd and mutinous libels" which had been posted around London. One libel was found on the property of a Dutch Church and contained violent anti-foreigner sentiments and multiple allusions to the works of Marlowe.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-8) The next day, Kyd was among those arrested; he would later believe that he had been the victim of an informer.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-:0-2) His lodgings were searched and instead of evidence of the "libels" there was found an [Arianist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arianism "Arianism") tract, described by an investigator as "vile heretical conceits denying the eternal deity of [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ "Jesus Christ") found amongst the papers of Thos. Kydd \[ _[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic "Sic")_\], prisoner ... which he affirmeth he had from C. Marley \[ _[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic "Sic")_\]". Historians such as [Frederick Boas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Boas "Frederick Boas") believe that Kyd was tortured brutally to obtain this information.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-:0-2) Kyd told authorities the writings found in his possession belonged to Christopher Marlowe, a fellow dramatist and former roommate. Kyd "accused his former roommate of being a blasphemous traitor, an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist "Atheist") who believed that Jesus Christ was a [homosexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexual "Homosexual"),"[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-9) an uninformed confusion over the Arian and early [Gnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnostic "Gnostic") concept of [homoousios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homoousios "Homoousios"). Following the accusation, Marlowe was summoned by the Privy Council and, while waiting for a decision on his case, was killed in an incident in [Deptford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deptford "Deptford") involving known government agents.

Kyd was eventually released but was not accepted back into his lord's service. Believing he was under suspicion of atheism himself, he wrote to the [Lord Keeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Keeper "Lord Keeper"), Sir John Puckering, protesting his innocence, but his efforts to clear his name were apparently fruitless. The last we hear from the playwright is the publication of _Cornelia_ early in 1594. In the dedication to the [Countess of Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridget_(Morrison)_Radcliffe_(Countess_of_Sussex) "Bridget (Morrison) Radcliffe (Countess of Sussex)") he alludes to the "bitter times and privy broken passions" he had endured. Kyd died later that year at the age of 35, and was buried on 15 August in [St Mary Colechurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary_Colechurch "St Mary Colechurch") in London. In December of that same year, Kyd's mother legally renounced the administration of his estate, probably because it was debt-ridden.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-:0-2)

St Mary Colechurch was destroyed in the [Great Fire of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_London "Great Fire of London") in 1666, and not rebuilt.

## Works

The dates of composition are approximate.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kyd#cite_note-10)

- _Don Horatio_ (partially extant in _The First Part of Hieronimo_, _c_. 1586)
- _[The Spanish Tragedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spanish_Tragedy "The Spanish Tragedy")_ ( _c_. 1587)
- _The Householder's Philosophy_ (translation, 1588)
- _[The Murder of John Brewen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Murder_of_John_Brewen "The Murder of John Brewen")_ (pamphlet, 1592)

- _[Cornelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelia_(play) "Cornelia (play)")_ (translation of [Robert Garnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Garnier "Robert Garnier"), 1594)
