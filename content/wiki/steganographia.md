Steganographia (book)

_**Steganographia**_ is a book on [steganography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steganography "Steganography"), written in c. 1499 by the German [Benedictine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict "Order of Saint Benedict") abbot and polymath [Johannes Trithemius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Trithemius "Johannes Trithemius").

## General

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Steganographia&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: General")\]

Trithemius' most famous work, _Steganographia_ (written c.1499; published [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt "Frankfurt"), 1606), was placed on the _[Index Librorum Prohibitorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_Librorum_Prohibitorum "Index Librorum Prohibitorum")_ in 1609[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steganographia#cite_note-1) and removed in 1900.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steganographia#cite_note-2) This book is in three volumes, and appears to be about [magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_(paranormal) "Magic (paranormal)")—specifically, about using spirits to communicate over long distances. However, since the publication of a decryption [key](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_(cryptography) "Key (cryptography)") to the first two volumes in 1606, they were discovered to be actually concerned with [cryptography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptography "Cryptography") and steganography. Until 1996, the third volume was widely believed to be solely about magic, but the "magical" formulas have now been shown to be [covertexts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stegotext "Stegotext") for yet more material on cryptography.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steganographia#cite_note-3)[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steganographia#cite_note-4)

## Reception

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Steganographia&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Reception")\]

References within the third book to magical works by such figures as [Agrippa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Cornelius_Agrippa "Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa") and [John Dee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dee "John Dee") still lend credence to the idea of a mystic-magical foundation of the third volume.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steganographia#cite_note-5)[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steganographia#cite_note-6) Additionally, while Trithemius's steganographic methods can be established to be free of the need for angelic–astrological mediation, an underlying theological motive for their contrivance remains. The preface to _[Polygraphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygraphia_(book) "Polygraphia (book)")_ equally establishes the everyday practicability of cryptography, and was conceived by Trithemius as a "secular consequent of the ability of a soul specially empowered by God to reach, by magical means, from earth to Heaven".[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steganographia#cite_note-7) [Robert Hooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hooke "Robert Hooke") suggested, in the chapter o _f Dr. Dee's Book of Spirits_, that John Dee used Trithemian steganography to conceal his communication with [Queen Elizabeth I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England "Elizabeth I of England").[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steganographia#cite_note-8)
