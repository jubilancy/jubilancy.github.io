Little Birds

_**Little Birds**_ is [Anaïs Nin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana%C3%AFs_Nin "Anaïs Nin")'s second published work of [erotica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erotic_literature "Erotic literature"), which appeared in 1979 two years after her death,[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Birds_(short_story_collection)#cite_note-1)
but was apparently written in the early 1940s when she was part of a group "writing pornography for a dollar a day."[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Birds_(short_story_collection)#cite_note-2)

The book is a collection of thirteen short stories. The sexual topics covered are quite varied, ranging from [pedophilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedophilia "Pedophilia") to [lesbianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbianism "Lesbianism"), but linked by an interest in female subjectivity[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Birds_(short_story_collection)#cite_note-3) and in the dialectic of discourse and intercourse.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Birds_(short_story_collection)#cite_note-4) Many of the same characters that appear in _[Delta of Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_of_Venus "Delta of Venus")_, her first published book of erotica, reappear here.

## Title and themes

The 'little birds' of the title story refer both to the actual birds used by its [exhibitionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibitionism "Exhibitionism") protagonist to [attract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grooming_(sexology) "Grooming (sexology)") young schoolgirls to his attic, and (metaphorically) to the girls' flight when he finally [exposes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indecent_exposure "Indecent exposure") himself.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Birds_(short_story_collection)#cite_note-5)

In other stories, Nin calls into question the objectifying tendencies of the [male gaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Male_gaze "Male gaze"); both male and female complicity in [masochism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-defeating_personality_disorder "Self-defeating personality disorder"); and the pornographic genre itself through her subtle subversion.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Birds_(short_story_collection)#cite_note-6)

The contents of the book include a "Preface" and the thirteen short stories: "Little Birds", "The Woman on the Dune", "Lina", "Two Sisters", "Sirocco", "The Maja", "A Model", "The Queen", "Hilda and Rango", "The Chanchiquito", "Saffron", "Mandra", and "Runaway".

## Conditions of writing

While writing her erotica, and organising that of her fellow writers such as [George Barker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Barker_(poet) "George Barker (poet)"), Nin referred to herself jokingly as the "madam of this snobbish literary house of prostitution" for a client who examined sexual activity "to the exclusion of aspects which are the fuel that ignites it. Intellectual, imaginative, romantic, emotional."[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Birds_(short_story_collection)#cite_note-Delta_Preface-7) In her 1976 preface to _Delta of Venus_ she said "I had a feeling that Pandora's box contained the mysteries of woman's sensuality, so different from man's and for which man's language was inadequate.... Here in the erotica I was writing to entertain, under pressure from a client who wanted me to 'leave out the poetry.' I believed that my style was derived from a reading of men's works. For this reason I long felt that I had compromised my feminine self. I put the erotica aside. Rereading it these many years later, I see that my own voice was not completely suppressed. In numerous passages I was intuitively using a woman's language, seeing sexual experience from a woman's point of view. I finally decided to release the erotica for publication because it shows the beginning efforts of a woman in a world that had been the domain of men."[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Birds_(short_story_collection)#cite_note-Delta_Preface-7)

[Susie Bright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susie_Bright "Susie Bright") saw the results as forming part of a pioneering feminist erotica.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Birds_(short_story_collection)#cite_note-8)

In 2021 [Sky Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Atlantic "Sky Atlantic") released a six-part erotic drama based on Little Birds.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Birds_(short_story_collection)#cite_note-9)
