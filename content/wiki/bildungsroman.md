In  [literary criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_criticism "Literary criticism"), a  **bildungsroman**  (German pronunciation:  [[ˈbɪldʊŋs.ʁoˌmaːn]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German "Help:IPA/Standard German")  [ⓘ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:De-Bildungsroman.ogg "File:De-Bildungsroman.ogg")) is a  [literary genre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_genre "Literary genre")  that focuses on the psychological and moral growth and change of the  [protagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protagonist "Protagonist")  from childhood to adulthood ([coming of age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coming_of_age "Coming of age")).[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-FOOTNOTELynch1999-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBakhtin199621-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJeffers20052-3)[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-4)[[a]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-6)  The term comes from the German words  _[Bildung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildung "Bildung")_  ('formation' or 'education') and  _Roman_  ('novel').

## Origin

The term was coined in 1819 by  [philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology "Philology")  [Johann Karl Simon Morgenstern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Morgenstern "Karl Morgenstern")  in his university lectures, and was later famously reprised by  [Wilhelm Dilthey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Dilthey "Wilhelm Dilthey"), who legitimized it in 1870 and popularized it in 1905.[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEngel2008263%E2%80%93266-5)[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-FOOTNOTESummerfieldDownward20101-7)  The genre is further characterized by a number of formal, topical, and thematic features.[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-8)  The term  _coming-of-age novel_  is sometimes used interchangeably with bildungsroman, but its use is usually wider and less technical.

The birth of the bildungsroman is normally dated to the publication of  _[Wilhelm Meister's Apprenticeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Meister%27s_Apprenticeship "Wilhelm Meister's Apprenticeship")_  by  [Johann Wolfgang von Goethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Wolfgang_von_Goethe "Johann Wolfgang von Goethe")  in 1795–96,[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJeffers200549-9)  or sometimes to  [Christoph Martin Wieland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Martin_Wieland "Christoph Martin Wieland")'s  _Geschichte des Agathon_  of 1767.[[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-Swales,_Martin_1978-10)  Although the bildungsroman arose in Germany, it has had extensive influence first in Europe and later throughout the world.  [Thomas Carlyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Carlyle "Thomas Carlyle")'s English translation of Goethe's novel (1824) and his own  _[Sartor Resartus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sartor_Resartus "Sartor Resartus")_  (1833–34), the first English bildungsroman, inspired many British novelists.[[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-11)[[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-12)[[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-Golban-13)  In the 20th century, it spread to France[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-14)[[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-15)  and several other countries around the globe.[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-16)

Barbara Whitman noted that the  _[Iliad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iliad "Iliad")_  might be the first bildungsroman. It is not just "the story of the Trojan War. The Trojan War is in effect the backdrop for the story of  [Achilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achilles "Achilles")' development. At the beginning Achilles is still a rash youth, making rash decisions which cost dearly to himself and all around him. (...) The story reaches its conclusion when Achilles has reached maturity and allows King Priam to recover Hector's body".[[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-17)

The genre translates fairly directly into the cinematic form, the  [coming-of-age film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coming-of-age_film "Coming-of-age film").

## Plot outline

A bildungsroman is a growing up or "coming of age" of a generally naive person who goes in search of answers to life's questions expecting that these will gain him or her experience of the world. The genre evolved from  [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore "Folklore")  tales of a  [dunce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunce "Dunce")  or youngest child going out in the world to seek their fortune.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-18)  Usually in the beginning of the story, there is an emotional loss that makes the protagonist leave on his journey. In a bildungsroman, the goal is maturity, and the protagonist achieves it gradually and with difficulty. The genre often features a main conflict between the main character and society. Typically, the values of society are gradually accepted by the  [protagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protagonist "Protagonist"), who is ultimately accepted into society—the protagonist's mistakes and disappointments are over. In some works, the protagonist is able to reach out and help others after having achieved maturity.

[Franco Moretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Moretti "Franco Moretti")  "argues that the main conflict in the bildungsroman is the myth of  [modernity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernity "Modernity")  with its overvaluation of youth and progress as it clashes with the static  [teleological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleological "Teleological")  vision of happiness and reconciliation found in the endings of  [Goethe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goethe "Goethe")'s  _Wilhelm Meister_  and even  [Jane Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Austen "Jane Austen")'s  _[Pride and Prejudice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_and_Prejudice_(novel) "Pride and Prejudice (novel)")_".[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-19)

There are many variations and subgenres of bildungsroman that focus on the growth of an individual. An  _Entwicklungsroman_  ('development novel') is a story of general growth rather than self-cultivation. An  _Erziehungsroman_  ("education novel") focuses on training and formal schooling,[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-20)  while a  _[Künstlerroman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BCnstlerroman "Künstlerroman")_  ("artist novel") is about the development of an artist and shows a growth of the self.[[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-Werlock2010p387-21)  Furthermore, some memoirs and published journals can be regarded as bildungsroman although claiming to be predominantly factual (e.g.,  _[The Dharma Bums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dharma_Bums "The Dharma Bums")_  by  [Jack Kerouac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Kerouac "Jack Kerouac")  or _[The Motorcycle Diaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Motorcycle_Diaries_(book) "The Motorcycle Diaries (book)")_  by  [Ernesto "Che" Guevara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che_Guevara "Che Guevara")).[[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-22)  The term is also more loosely used to describe coming-of-age films and related works in other genres.

## Notes

1.  **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_ref-6 "Jump up")**  Engel explains that the term has in recent years been applied to very different novels but originally meant a novel of formation of a character, of an individual personality on interaction (including conflict) with society. He also points out that it was, like the "novel of education" (_Erziehungsroman_), a subgenre of the "novel of development" (_Entwicklungsroman_).[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEngel2008263%E2%80%93266-5)
2.  **[^](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bildungsroman?useskin=vector#cite_ref-74 "Jump up")**  Back of the French translation in the "Folio" collection (éditions Gallimard, 2010):  _"Avec ce roman d'apprentissage, Philip Roth poursuit son analyse de l'histoire de l'Amérique – celle des années cinquante, des tabous et des frustrations sexuelles – et de son impact sur la vie d'un homme jeune, isolé, vulnérable"_.
